Mind the S-1: Anthropic is telling investors its own AI might do things nobody asked it to

Anthropic's S-1 warns that its models may take harmful actions no user directed, and that its safeguards may not always stop people from misusing them. The same document asks the public to buy shares. It goes on to describe a business that depends on scarce chips and power, defends copyright suits, has taken the Department of War to court, and leaves control with a trust and the seven co-founders.

A $104.0 billion loss: $94.3 billion of it comes from the notes gaining value

The operating loss in the first nine months of 2026 was $1.3 billion. Most of the rest sits below the operating line: a $94.3 billion non-cash charge for the higher fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs, which the filing ties to increases in Anthropic's own valuation, and a new $10.4 billion of other expense, driven mainly by fair-value accounting for its partner financing arrangements.

Through the clouds: close to half of revenue reaches Anthropic through cloud marketplaces

Read these fee ranges with care

The filing says "channel partners", and those aren't only clouds

  • Fees may also go to systems integrators and other partners, and may attach to sales outside the marketplaces. p.3, 147
  • The filing doesn't explain the jump from about $16–$18 to about $33–$35. It does say sales and marketing fell from 22% of revenue in Q1 2026 to 14% in Q3, partly because channel-partner terms changed. p.162
  • The ranges assume all of the increase belongs to marketplace sales, and that prior-period fees fall between zero and all of that period's sales and marketing line. p.23

View 06 · Who decides
Archive · written before the IPO

Who decides: a trust picks the board majority and seven founders hold 50.1% of the vote

Anthropic is a Delaware public benefit corporation, so its directors must weigh a stated Company Purpose alongside returns to stockholders. The Long-Term Benefit Trust elects four of the seven directors. A single Class F share, held through the co-founders' LLC, carries 50.1% of the general vote. Nasdaq will treat Anthropic as a controlled company.

Litigation

Most of the open cases are about training data

  • Bartz (authors, books): settled for $1.5B plus interest, approved July 2026. Authors and publishers who opted out have filed their own suits. p.F-59–F-60
  • Concord I (song lyrics): cross-motions for summary judgment heard October 21, 2026. p.F-59

  • Reddit: narrowed in September 2026 to contract, interference and unfair competition. p.F-60
  • Canadian actions, including La Presse and an October 2026 claim by Ontario news publishers; consumer class actions over subscription advertising. p.F-59–F-60
  • Antitrust: a September 2026 putative class action claims frontier developers agreed to slow model development, under Sherman Act §1. Risk 59, p.98
Government and national security

An appeals court upheld one supply-chain-risk designation, 2–1

  • Federal agencies were directed to stop using the technology, and the Department of War designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk" (letters of March 4, 2026). p.73, 245
  • The N.D. Cal. largely granted summary judgment for Anthropic on August 27, 2026. p.73
  • The D.C. Circuit upheld the Section 4713 designation 2–1 on September 25, 2026; rehearing en banc requested. p.73
  • Commerce imposed worldwide export restrictions on Fable 5 and Mythos 5 in June 2026, later lifted. p.73, 100
  • An FTC civil investigative demand in October 2026 covering consumer protection, privacy and product safety. State attorney general inquiries continue. Risk 15, p.52; F-60

What this rests on
  • The filing gives no seat counts and no split by plan.
  • The prices and mixes in this table are modeling assumptions. Prices sit inside today's public list: Pro $17–$20, Max $100, Ultra $200, Team Standard $20–$25, Team Premium $100–$125, Enterprise $20 a seat plus usage at API rates. They are applied to the first nine months of 2026, when plans and prices may have differed.
  • Treating Enterprise seats this way isn't confirmed by the filing. The model counts the $20 seat fee as subscription revenue and leaves usage out.
  • The model assumes 12 seats per Team organization and 250 per Enterprise organization.
  • Don't subtract these cases from the 40 million. That number is a trailing count of a different group, and these cases come from a nine-month average.
  • The $771 million is a flat average of nine months, and revenue rose fast across them. Q3 alone works out to about $1.24 billion a month (the amendment's nine-month figure less the original S-1's first-half figure), so a current count would run higher.   • Results now run through the nine months ended September 30, 2026. The third quarter shows a GAAP operating loss of $567 million, driven mainly by $5.0 billion of short-term compute. p.23, 138, 162
