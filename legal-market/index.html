Lawyers reach Claude two ways: by buying it, or through the legal tools they already use.

Named firms, scale and limits

Freshfields runs Claude on its own platform, and it is not a US firm. Quinn Emanuel has a partner-built litigation platform; how it pays for Claude is not stated. Kirkland & Ellis plans to spend $500M over three to four years on its own AI platform, Reuters reported, and declined to say whether it would rely on a specific model. None of that is counted as Claude spend.

$350K a year across a firm of 1,805 people firmwide is about $16 a person a month. Across the 1,444 assumed to be in the US, the count used for whole-firm seats, it is about $20. The S-1/A puts Enterprise users without Claude Code at under $75 a month. The per-firm figures cover what a firm pays for Claude itself and leave out developers and hosting.

A firm's own tools count whether or not it also buys seats. Nothing comes off for offices outside the US, so spend at global firms may run high. People using tools their firm built appear in no user row, which leaves the user count short by an unknown amount.

Reading the bars, and what is kept off

Read the bars one at a time

They mix fiscal-year revenue with ARR (annual recurring revenue), and different dates and regions, and they overlap, so they do not add up. Vendor ARR may include money that ends up with Claude. On assumed shares, about $64.2M a year of global spend reaches Claude through legal vendors in the Base case. The US part, about $46M, is in the Claude mark and is also inside those vendors' own bars, so the mark and the bars overlap.

Kept off the bars

Westlaw and CoCounsel product revenue is not disclosed. No reliable total exists for contract lifecycle management (CLM) software, and published estimates run about $1.1B to $2.3B. Docusign IAM is about $353M, calculated, global and broader than CLM. Icertis was approaching $350M ARR in Aug 2025, reported by Sacra.

Bigger numbers for context

Gartner forecast in 2024 that global legal tech would reach $50B by 2027. Menlo puts enterprise legal AI apps at $650M in 2025, leaving out general assistants, and Menlo lists Claude as a portfolio company. The Census puts US legal services revenue at $447.4B in 2025.

How the Base case is built

The total is five counts added together, four on the direct route and one on the vendor route. Each narrows a starting group of people or a revenue figure through a chain of assumptions, and the notes listed below give them.

  1. 1

    Law firms

    How

    US firms in five sizes × share using or exploring Claude × a discount because survey respondents chose to answer × share buying seats × seats per firm. 80% of seats count as users.

  2. 2

    Individuals

    How

    Legal professionals × share using AI × share choosing Claude × share paying for themselves. Spend counts only the part of the subscription used for legal work.

  3. 3

    In-house and staff

    How

    In-house, compliance, legal operations and government staff, split by who bought access.

  4. 4

    US API

    How

    Firms running their own Claude tools × Claude spend per firm, plus tools legal departments build, at an assumed US share.

  5. 5

    Vendors

    How

    Vendor revenue × model costs × Claude's share, cut to the US at 72.2%. Harvey and Legora claimed users × Claude's share, at the same 72.2%.

  6. 6

    Total

    How

    Steps 1 to 4 are the direct route, and step 5 is the vendor route.

What the sliders move, and where they stop

Adjusted results are arithmetic on assumed inputs. The first four sliders move only direct figures and the two vendor sliders only vendor figures. Claude's share moves vendor spend and vendor users, but model costs moves vendor spend alone. Law-firm seats are counted by firm size, which no slider touches. Every other input, including the 72.2% US share, stays at the loaded case's value. The four direct sliders run past the Low and High values. The vendor sliders stop at the low and high in Note 8. Loaded on Base they run from $20.0M to $150.8M and never reach $201M, which also needs $800M of revenue at other vendors.
